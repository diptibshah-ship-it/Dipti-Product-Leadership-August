# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution
>
> Your personal reflection on building the strategy end to end.

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> For Project Meridian the hardest part was aligning diverse stakeholder expectations while we were still validating core user needs. Different teams framed success differently (growth, retention, operational efficiency), which made prioritization noisy. I worked through it by running focused stakeholder interviews, documenting assumptions and desired outcomes, and creating a simple prioritization framework tied to a clear North Star metric for Meridian. We used rapid prototypes and short experiments to surface data quickly and used AI tools to synthesize research notes and draft experiment designs — that combination helped turn subjective opinions into testable hypotheses and sped decision-making.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course overall._

> 1. Framing matters more than feature lists: when we reframed Meridian around the customer's job-to-be-done, previously competing ideas converged into a cohesive roadmap.
> 2. Small experiments win: low-cost, frequent experiments revealed signal faster than long specification cycles and prevented wasted engineering effort.
> 3. AI is a force-multiplier: generative tools accelerated research synthesis, user interview note-taking, and draft communications, allowing the team to iterate more quickly and surface insights that would have taken much longer manually.

## "Aha!" moment

_Your main "aha" moment during the project process._

> The main "aha" for Project Meridian came when we stopped optimizing for feature parity with competitors and instead optimized for end-to-end orchestration of the user's workflow. That shift exposed a smaller set of high-leverage capabilities that delivered disproportionately better outcomes for users and clearer KPIs for the business. Once we focused on that orchestration, buy-in from stakeholders became easier and the roadmap tightened around measurable impact.
