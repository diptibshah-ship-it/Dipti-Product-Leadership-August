# Product Strategy One-Pager & OKRs: Meridian Foundations (B2B · adoption + expansion)

> Module 1 · Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, ★ Deliverable 1
>
> Your one spine: the **Playing to Win** cascade, one deliberate **hard no**, and an **OKR cascade** that flows directly from it.
> Draft the cascade + hard no in Sprint 1, then add the OKRs in Sprint 2. The goal: specific enough that a skeptical board member couldn't poke a hole in it.

## 0. Chosen scenario

**Path:** Meridian Foundations (B2B · adoption + expansion)

We picked Meridian because the biggest near-term opportunity is driving faster product adoption and predictable account expansion in the mid-market (SaaS) segment: customers who will pay more when they see clear time-to-value and embedded expansion paths.

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics | Customers choose Meridian as the fastest, lowest-risk way to go from evaluation → production, and then expand usage across teams; we measure winning as customers achieving production launch within 30 days and expanding to 3+ internal teams within 12 months. |
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | Mid-market B2B SaaS vendors (ARR $1M–$50M) in North America and EU, developer/engineering-led adoption with CS-assisted expansion. Channels: product-led self-serve + direct sales for expansion; partner integrations with adjacent platform vendors. We will not invest first in large-enterprise procurement motions, vertical-specific customizations, or consumer segments. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | A turnkey foundations product with production-ready integrations, SDKs, and automated provisioning that materially shortens time-to-first-value; paired with data-driven CS playbooks that convert activation into expansion. We win on reduced integration cost, predictable TTV, and measurable in-product triggers for expansion. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | 1) Developer-first SDKs & APIs; 2) Out-of-the-box integrations for top 10 partner ecosystems; 3) Instrumentation & in-product analytics for activation/expansion signals; 4) Scalable customer success playbooks and enablement for expansion; 5) GTM alignment (sales + product + CS). Build core product; partner for niche integrations; buy/extend analytics where sensible. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | Weekly growth cadence focused on: Time-to-First-Value (median), Activation Rate (day-7), Expansion Rate (customers expanding to additional seats/modules within 6 months), CAC payback (90-day target), and NRR. Monthly cross-functional expansion reviews with CS + Sales + Product to surface accounts and run expansion experiments. Quarterly roadmap gating against TTFV and activation impact. |

## 2. Your one hard no

_One valuable thing you are explicitly choosing **not** to do, and why it protects the focus of everything above. This is a deliberate trade-off, not a backlog of deprioritized items._

> We will not build bespoke, high-touch professional services or deep enterprise-only customizations as a primary revenue path in the next 12 months, because doing so shifts engineering and CS effort away from productizing onboarding and expansion levers that scale across many mid-market accounts.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone._

> **Objective:** Rapidly increase adoption and predictable expansion among mid-market customers so Meridian becomes the default foundations platform for mid-market SaaS.
>
> - **KR1:** Increase product Activation Rate (customers who complete core onboarding and show sustained usage at day 14) from 35% → 55% by 2026-12-31.
> - **KR2:** Reduce median Time-to-First-Value (from signup to first production-ready integration) from 21 days → 7 days by 2026-12-31.
> - **KR3:** Increase Net Revenue Retention (NRR) from 100% → 115% by 2026-12-31, driven by account expansions (upsells and cross-sells) rather than price changes.

(Notes: baselines above are placeholders — replace with your actuals if available. KRs are outcome metrics customers and the board care about, not feature checklists.)

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

| The "no" I should reconsider | Excluding enterprise/custom work entirely might close large near-term bookings that improve cash flow. | Defend: permit a small, tightly scoped pilot program for strategic enterprise logos, but require a clear productization roadmap and SLA for turning pilots into product features. |
| Strategy or wish list? Why? | Strategy: choices are coherent — target segment, TTFV-led value proposition, and CS-enabled expansion all reinforce each other. Risk becomes a wish list if we add too many product verticals or enterprise custom work. | Keep the hard no and require every new ask to map to one of the core KRs before approval. |
