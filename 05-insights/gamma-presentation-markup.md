### Section B: Sequencing Rationale & Trade-offs

**Headline (Orange):**
# Why We Prioritized This Way

**Trade-Off Memo:**

**✓ What We Sequenced FIRST: SDK/Integration Acceleration**

*Why?*
- Blocks downstream work; highest customer friction point
- TTFV reduction is our primary value proposition
- Cost of delay is HIGH: every week we delay compounds the gap
- Early wins build momentum and sales proof points

**→ What We Pushed Out: Enterprise-Only Feature Parity Work**

*Why?*
- Misaligns with our mid-market strategy
- Would dilute engineering focus away from activation levers
- Enterprise features can be added later if we prove the mid-market motion
- Preserves team velocity on core bets

**✗ What We Cut Entirely: Vertical-Specific Compliance Builds**

*Why?*
- Falls outside our target segment (mid-market SaaS doesn't require HIPAA-level compliance)
- Would delay core TTFV wins by 6–8 weeks
- 1–2 large enterprise opportunities don't move our NRR metric
- Expanding existing 50 mid-market customers compounds value faster

**Decision Framework: WSJF**
- **Weighted**: Prioritized by TTFV impact (broadest customer reach)
- **Smallest Job First**: SDK integrations are bounded; ship fast
- **Minimize Cost of Delay**: Every week matters in adoption race

### Column 1: Friction Points

**Sub-headline (Orange):**
## 🔧 Friction Points

**Question:** *What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?*

**Content:**

The biggest challenge was **aligning diverse stakeholder expectations while we were still validating core user needs.**

Different teams framed success differently:
- **Sales:** Growth metrics (MRR ramp, customer acquisition)
- **CS:** Retention & NRR (expanding existing revenue)
- **Engineering:** Reliability & SDKs (reducing technical debt)
- **Product:** Activation & TTFV (reducing customer friction)

**How We Worked Through It:**

We grounded everyone in a single North Star: **the customer's job-to-be-done.**

The question: *"What is the core job our customer is hiring us for?"*

Answer: *"Go from evaluation to production in 30 days, then expand across their team."*

Once we reframed around this, all the competing success metrics aligned:
- Sales wins when we ship fast (TTFV)
- CS wins when customers activate (expansion follows)
- Engineering wins when SDKs are reliable (reduces support load)
- Product wins when activation unlocks expansion (NRR grows)

**Insight:** Framing is the unlock. When you find the shared job-to-be-done, the rest cascades.

---

### Column 2: Key Learnings

**Sub-headline (Teal):**
## 💡 Key Learnings

**Question:** *A few surprising discoveries or insights you gained from the course.*

**Learning #1: Framing Matters More Than Feature Lists**

When we reframed Meridian around the **customer's job-to-be-done**, previously competing ideas converged into a cohesive roadmap.

We weren't trying to build everything; we were solving one problem really well.

→ **Applied to roadmap:** Every Now/Next/Later item traces back to one of three OKRs.

---

**Learning #2: Small Experiments Win**

Low-cost, frequent experiments revealed signal faster than long specification cycles and prevented wasted engineering effort.

We didn't have to build the perfect SDK—we shipped an MVP, ran 3 customer pilots in 2 weeks, incorporated feedback, shipped v2.

Cost: 2 weeks of eng time
Value unlocked: Saved 8 weeks of speccing + building the wrong thing

→ **Applied to execution:** Weekly cadence, bi-weekly prioritization, monthly retrospectives keep us tight.

---

**Learning #3: AI is a Force Multiplier**

Generative tools (synthesis, note-taking, draft communications) accelerated research synthesis and allowed the team to iterate more quickly and surface insights at scale.

We used AI to:
- Synthesize 20 customer interviews into 5 key themes (4 hours → 30 min)
- Draft expansion playbooks and test them against customer data
- Generate onboarding copy variants and A/B test them

→ **Applied to execution:** Every weekly cadence includes "insights harvest" using AI synthesis tools.

---

### Column 3: Aha Moment

**Sub-headline (Purple):**
## 🎯 The Aha Moment

**Question:** *Your main "aha" moment during the project process.*

**The Shift That Changed Everything:**

**From:** Optimizing for feature parity with competitors

**To:** Optimizing for end-to-end orchestration of the user's workflow

---

**What This Meant:**

We stopped asking: *"What features do competitors have that we're missing?"*

We started asking: *"What is the user's full workflow from 'considering our product' to 'successfully deployed in production' to 'expanding to other teams'? How do we orchestrate every step of that journey?"*

**The Result:**

That single shift exposed what customers really wanted—a cohesive experience across evaluation, onboarding, and expansion—not a feature checklist.

It meant:
- ✓ Investing in automation and self-serve (not professional services)
- ✓ Building integrations for the ecosystems our customers already use
- ✓ Measuring success by TTFV and Activation (not feature count)
- ✓ Empowering CS with playbooks, not adding more sales reps

**Why It Mattered:**

This reframe became the north star for every prioritization decision. When roadmap debates surfaced, we'd ask: "Does this improve the end-to-end workflow?" If not, we cut it.

It unified the team. It protected the focus. It made the strategy defensible.
