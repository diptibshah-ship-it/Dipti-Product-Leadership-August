# Product Strategy One-Pager & OKRs: Meridian (Field Adoption)

> Module 1 · Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, ★ Deliverable 1
>
> Your one spine: the **Playing to Win** cascade, one deliberate **hard no**, and an **OKR cascade** that flows directly from it.
> Draft the cascade + hard no in Sprint 1, then add the OKRs in Sprint 2. The goal: specific enough that a skeptical board member couldn't poke a hole in it.

## 0. Chosen scenario

**Path:** _Meridian Foundations (B2B · adoption + expansion)_

_Target: close the gap that prevents field teams from making Meridian their daily workflow; focus on field adoption for commercial construction projects._

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics | (Success for superintendents will be like documenting work in seconds without logging in to a computer — capture photos from a phone, add voice notes, and not have to enter the same information multiple times.) |
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | Targeting field superintendents and workers actively working on commercial construction projects; mobile-first on-site capture (photo, voice, quick updates); pilots in North American mid-market GC accounts. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | Mobile-first experience with one-tap photos, voice notes, robust offline mode, and AI-generated reports that reduce admin time for field teams. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | Mobile UX and performance, offline sync and conflict resolution, AI automation for report generation, enterprise integrations (PMIS/ERP), and embedded user research with field teams. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | Weekly Active Users (field), median reporting time per update, feature adoption (photo/voice/AI-report usage), NPS for field users, and a monthly field-research ritual (ride-alongs/pilot retrospectives). |

## 2. Your one hard no

_One valuable thing you are explicitly choosing **not** to do, and why it protects the focus of everything above. This is a deliberate trade-off, not a backlog of deprioritized items._

> We will not redesign the enterprise reporting suite or shift the roadmap to be compliance-focused only; instead we will prioritize improvements that drive field adoption and make Meridian the default day-to-day workflow for on-site teams.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone._

> **Objective:** Make Meridian the default daily workflow for field teams
>
> - **KR1:** Weekly Active Field Users — from 35% to 75% (baseline to target)
> - **KR2:** Median reporting time per field update — from 8 minutes to < 2 minutes
> - **KR3:** Share of project updates recorded in Meridian — from 25% to 80%

(If you want dates, we can add target dates such as by the end of Q4 or by 2026-12-31.)

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

_Run the devil's-advocate prompt (in the Sprint 2 guide). Capture the verdict._

| Prompt question | What the AI surfaced | Change or defend? |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest assumption that could be wrong | Better mobile UX alone will drive field adoption | Change: run a small pilot and field research to validate adoption barriers (training, culture, connectivity, incentives). Defend: keep field adoption as the strategic focus rather than a full enterprise redesign. |
| The board question I can't yet answer | Will enterprise buyers enable/mandate field use of Meridian, or will field teams need additional incentives and training? | Change: surface pilot results and adopters' reasons; add stakeholder engagement plan. |
| KRs that are outputs in disguise | Risk that "reporting time" improvements could be achieved by scripted shortcuts rather than genuine adoption | Change: measure adoption signals (active users, frequency, diverse feature usage) alongside time metrics. |
| The "no" I should reconsider | If compliance or reporting requirements become a blocker for buyers, the "no" may need revisiting | Defend: prioritize field adoption now and re-evaluate only if customer procurement or compliance requirements demand it. |
| Strategy or wish list? Why? | Strategy if supported by validated pilots and tracked metrics; wish list if it's only feature-led without behavior validation | Change: run pilots + weekly metrics to prove causality; defend the focus on field workflows as the true business lever. |
